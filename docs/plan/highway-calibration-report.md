# Highway factor calibration report (checklist 3.7, R10)

Generated by `npm run calibrate:highway` on 2026-11-16 from the data build (84 variants, **14** with both an NRCan highway rating and a tested mild-weather highway figure, B48).

**What the factor does.** A car with no tested highway figure plans with NRCan highway × factor (route-planner.md §3). The planner turns that into battery % used, so the figure it needs is energy taken **from the battery** at 110–120 km/h.

**Why it may come out below 1.15.** The 1.15 guess assumed NRCan's lab cycle is slower than real highway driving, which is true. But NRCan figures are **wall-side**: they include charging losses (roughly 10–15 %). The tested figures are mostly **battery-side** (the car's own display). The two effects partly cancel, and the fitted factor absorbs both.

**Error** below is NRCan × factor against the tested figure. **+** means the model assumes more energy than the test (the safe side: the car arrives with more battery than planned). **−** means less (the risky side).

## Caveats

- 14 cars, every tested value `low_confidence` (single tests, mostly battery-side, a few rounded). Speeds range from 70 mph (113 km/h) to 120 km/h.
- **Test speed matters.** The 120 km/h tests (Nyland) mostly sit above 1 and the 70 mph (113 km/h) US tests below it. Canadian highway limits are 100–110 km/h, but traffic often runs faster, so a factor a little above the best fit is the honest middle as well as the safer one.
- Cars **with** a tested figure plan with it directly; the factor only matters for the others.
- Weather, wheels and elevation are handled separately (B48: mild-weather baseline, conservative formulas for the rest).
