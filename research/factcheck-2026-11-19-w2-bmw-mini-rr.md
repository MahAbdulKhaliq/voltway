# Fact-check: batch-2026-11-19-w2-bmw-mini-rr.json (75 powertrains, about 14 hardware generations)

**Verdict: FIX.** `npx tsx scripts/add-facts.ts research/batch-2026-11-19-w2-bmw-mini-rr.json --check` passes (1408 variants, 534 sources after applying, nothing written). The i3 (60/94/120 Ah), i5 pack/DC/AC, iX 2022-25, i7 2023-26 and iX3 figures match their pages. The problems are the port and Supercharger values on 2026 i5, the MINI/Rolls-Royce/Countryman estimates, the 2026 iX and 2027 Spectre estimates that rest on a changed pack, and several labels.

Limits of this check: the WebFetch quota ran out part-way. I did NOT re-open the BMW US iX4 page, the BMW US iX5 page, or the BMW US i3 sedan page (their values are marked "not re-read" below). `press.bmwgroup.com` pages (MINI Cooper SE kit, Countryman PDF) redirect to the newsroom home and could not be read. The Rolls-Royce US charging page timed out. No curves are in this batch, so items 8, 9 and 13 do not apply.

## Fan-out answers

- **i3 Ah per year.** 60 Ah ids 2014-2017, 94 Ah ids 2017-2018 (i3 and i3s), 120 Ah ids 2019-2021. The split follows the BMW US 2017 page (94 Ah new for 2017, 60 Ah listed as 22 kWh gross / 19 kWh net) and the 2019 page (42.2 / 37.9, 352 V). Wikipedia's US EPA table shows 60 Ah only for 2016 and the 94 Ah for 2017. It does not show a 2017 US 60 Ah car, but the repo has a 2017 60 Ah id (CA/US) and the figures are the same hardware, so this is fine. No mid-generation change was found within each Ah group.
- **iX.** 2022-2025 xDrive50 / M60 (105.2 net, 111.5 gross, 200 kW) and xDrive40 (71 / 76.6, 150 kW) are right per the BMW iX launch page. 2026 xDrive45/60/M70 are a different pack (new cells, 94.8 / 109.1 / 108.9 on BMW's German-spec page; DC 175/195/195). The split is correct; the estimated fields on the 2026 cars are the issue (fix 5).
- **i7 2027 facelift.** Correctly split from 2023-26: BMW US page says 112.5 kWh net "maximum", 195 to 250 kW, 28 min, native NACS on every i7, gen6 cylindrical cells. Fixes 6 and 7 below.
- **i5 / i7 / iX as same-cell siblings of the i4.** i5 and i4 eDrive40 are the same pack (i5 83.9 gross / 81.2 usable; repo i4 2026 gross 84.3 / usable 81.3; both about 205 kW, about 400 V), so the i5 estimates pass rule 5. The i7 (107.8 gross) and iX (111.5 gross) are different packs from the i4 (83.9). The cited i4 page says only "5th generation BMW eDrive technology includes a high-voltage battery", nothing about the iX or i7 cells. So chemistry and voltage for i7 and iX rest on "same generation", not "same pack" (fix 4).

## Ports with a US and a CA value on one variant: do they need a market split?

Yes, for `bmw/i5/2026-m60-xdrive` (ports nacs for US, ccs1 for CA; Supercharger yes for US, via_adapter for CA). The data model has one value per variant field, so the merge would show a conflict. Put the US car on `nacs`/`yes` and the CA car on `ccs1`/`via_adapter` using the `variants` section (the id is one powertrain with both a CA and a US car). The CA M60 `ccs1` is itself an unsourced assumption: BMW Canada only says "all-new" 2026+ models have NACS. Either find a Canadian i5 page or label the CA values estimated with the stated basis, and say if unproven.
The 2026 xDrive40 (CA/US) and eDrive40 (US) are a different problem: the US cars built from March 2026 have NACS and earlier cars CCS1. A variant cannot split by build date. Owner decision: record `nacs` (current production, BMW Remote Software Update only needed on older cars), or `ccs1` with a note, or add a note on the car. The batch currently states `ccs1` and `via_adapter`, which is wrong for most 2026 US cars built after March.
