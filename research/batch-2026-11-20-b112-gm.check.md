## Answers to the riskiest points

1. **Heat pump `standard` on every Ultium car: partly supported, labelling is wrong.**
   - Recurrent (opened): Lyriq 2023+, Optiq 2025+, Escalade IQ 2025+, Vistiq 2026+, Blazer/Equinox/Silverado EV 2024+, Hummer EV pickup/SUV 2022+, Sierra EV 2024+, Bolt 2027+: "all trims and drivetrains", no exceptions listed. This covers Silverado/Sierra WT, Hummer 2022 Edition 1 and Equinox 1LT as far as a community list can. It does not say "standard" vs "optional", only that every trim has it.
   - Motor.com (opened): the page says "standard in its Ultium-based EVs" about the **energy-recovery system** and does not mention a heat pump. The batch quotes it as proof that the heat pump is standard, and labels it `manufacturer`. That is an inference, not a statement. Green Car Reports (opened) says GM "plans to include heat-pump technology in all of its current and upcoming Ultium-platform electric vehicles", which is closer. GM TechLink Escalade IQ page (opened) says Ultium Energy Recovery is "an innovative heat pump thermal system"; it does not speak to trims.
   - So `standard` rests on Recurrent (community, all trims) plus a GM lineup statement joined by inference. No source for any trim says it is optional or absent; a search for Silverado WT / Equinox 1LT found nothing either way (a GM service bulletin snippet lists the bevHEAT heat pump among 2024 Silverado EV WT features, but the trim split was not visible). Acceptable with `low_confidence: true` (already set), but see required changes 1 and 2.
2. **2027 GM NACS cars `ccs1_to_nacs_sold`: sold is the better-supported reading, but not proven.**
   - Notebookcheck (opened) does say "comes with a CCS adapter included in its price" for the 2027 Bolt. TeslaNorth (Oct 2025, pre-launch, from search snippet) says the same.
   - Against it: Charged EVs first drive (opened): "CCS-to-NACS ($189) are available from Chevrolet"; Electrek 2026-08-14 (opened) lists GM prices $189 / $67 / $256 and says buyers "will need a different adapter"; a search result shows GM's accessory page listing a CCS1 DC adapter at $189 with Bolt 2027 on its compatibility list (page not opened, blocked domain). Later and more specific sources say sold.
   - Electrek never says "no CCS1 adapter in the box" (the batch note says it does; Electrek is silent, it only implies it). The batch's note that the Notebookcheck page "was not opened" is now outdated: it was opened and does say "included".
   - Keep `ccs1_to_nacs_sold` with `low_confidence: true`; fix the note (change 3). The owner may want to confirm against a 2027 Bolt window sticker or GM order guide.
3. **Spark EV heat pump `none`: weak.** Recurrent says "no heat pump" (opened). The brief requires a spec list showing the system or similar; I found none (a search for a Spark EV PTC/heater spec turned up nothing). It stays `community` + `low_confidence`, which is honest, but it is not at the brief's bar. The Spark EV adapter `none` is well supported: GM TechLink "Spark EV models are excluded" (opened) and Teslarati says it "is simply not compatible" (opened).
4. **Celestiq heat pump `estimated`: acceptable.** Basis is GM's lineup statement for "all current and upcoming Ultium-platform" EVs (Green Car Reports) and TechLink listing Celestiq for the adapter. No Celestiq-specific page found (my search found none). Keep `estimated` + `low_confidence`, notes already say so. Not a rated-range estimate, so allowed.
5. **Canadian adapter entries applied to every GM model (US + CA cars, 2017-2026): acceptable as written.** Guide Auto (opened) names Blazer EV, Equinox EV, Silverado EV and Lyriq only, and says CA$310 "from a GM dealer". The batch records it as `nacs_to_ccs1_sold`, `low_confidence: true`, with a note that other models are assumed to follow the GM-wide programme. That is fair for Hummer/Sierra/Optiq/Escalade/Celestiq, but for Bolt EV/EUV 2017-2023 and Spark... (Spark gets `none` only) the extrapolation is weaker. Keep low_confidence; the notes on Bolt cars already say the article names four models only.
6. **Free adapter for 2020-22 Bolt EV / 2022 Bolt EUV: not supported.** What exists is a free **software update** at the dealer for "select" 2019-2020 Bolt EVs so the charge-port lock works with an adapter (InsideEVs, opened: "Bolts from other years should work without issue"). The adapter itself still had to be ordered; no source says it is free. The batch's `nacs_to_ccs1_sold` for these cars stands. Worth adding the software-update fact to the notes of 2019/2020 Bolt EV (optional). GM news later mentioned shipping the adapter to the home free for the rest of 2024 (search snippet only), which is not an adapter offer.
